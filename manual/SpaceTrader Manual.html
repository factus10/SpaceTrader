01Loading the program

SpaceTrader is a single TAP file of about 110K. It holds the BASIC program, two blocks of machine code and data, a font, the title screen, and twenty system illustrations — forty blocks in all, loaded as you need them.

From tape or an emulator

  1. Set the machine to Timex TS2068 or TC2068.
  2. Open spacetrader.tap.
  3. Type LOAD "" and press ENTER.

The program starts itself. Press any key at the title screen, then type a name for your ship and press ENTER. Pressing ENTER on its own names her Beowulf.

Leave fast loading onThe program pulls its own blocks off the tape as it runs — code, data and a picture for every system you visit. In an emulator, leave tape traps or fast loading enabled or every jump will take a minute.
Don't use RUNRUN starts the program without reserving the memory it needs. Always let it start itself, or GO TO 40 if you have stopped it.

02The screen

The top third of the screen is the world you are docked at. It stays there the whole time you are in port and changes when you jump.

Below it is the market. The header gives the system's name, its tech level and its law level — the two numbers that decide what everything costs and how much trouble you are in.

The ST column is what this port does with each good:

MarkMeansEffect on price
EXThe port exports it30% cheaper
IMThe port imports it40% dearer
--Neither—

An exclamation mark at the right of a line means that good is contraband here. The bottom line is always your credits, fuel, hull and heat.

Choosing

Each menu shows its keys with the letter in yellow. Press that letter. When a message appears it takes the place of the menu and shows in reverse — press any key to clear it and the menu comes back.

KeyDoesNotes
BBuyAsks which good, then how many tons.
SSellSame. You cannot sell what you do not hold.
JJumpLists the systems in reach.
PPort servicesFuel, repairs, licenses, mortgage.

03Trading

Buy where a good is exported and sell where it is imported. That is the whole engine. An exporter takes 30% off the base price and an importer adds 40%, so the spread on a full hold is real money.

Two other things move the price. A crowded world sells cheaper than an empty one, and a high-technology world sells its own manufactures cheaper than a backwater does. On top of all of it there is a swing of up to 15% either way, rolled afresh every time you dock. The same route pays differently each run, so a bad price is worth coming back for.

Your hold takes twenty tons. There is no part-load discount and no storage: everything you own is aboard.

The best single-jump run in the sector is luxuries from Ambergris into Junction Nine, which pays about 3,400 credits on a full hold. A typical profitable run pays about 1,200.

04The sector

Twenty systems. You can jump to anything within two columns and two rows, so a world two squares away on the diagonal is still one jump. Fuel costs one ton plus one for every two squares of distance — one to three tons a jump, out of a tank that holds eight.

The sector runs core to frontier, left to right. The core is high technology and high law: cheap fuel, cheap repairs, no pirates worth the name, and inspectors everywhere. The frontier is the reverse. Nothing legal is worth much out there, but nothing is illegal either, and the pirates know it.

Everything is reachable from Coriolis Gate, but not quickly. Ossuary and Vagrant are six jumps out, and Ossuary connects only through Kalideen — if you want the weapons there, you commit to the trip.

No refuelling in deep spaceThere is nowhere to buy fuel between systems. Check the tank before a long leg; being stranded with an empty hold and no credits ends the game.

05Contraband and licenses

Four goods are restricted, and the higher a world's law level the more it cares. A good is contraband anywhere whose law level is above its threshold.

GoodThresholdIllegal inLicense
Weapons49 of 201,440
Luxuries56 of 202,000
Electronics63 of 201,120
Medical72 of 20720

Buying contraband adds 2 to your heat. Carrying it is what customs are looking for.

A license, bought at any port, stops that good being contraband for you anywhere at all — the exclamation mark disappears from the market screen and customs stop caring. It is the expensive way to do what heat does cheaply and dangerously.

Which license firstWeapons is illegal in nine systems and costs 1,440. Luxuries is illegal in six and costs 2,000 but the goods themselves are worth far more. Medical at 720 is cheap because it is barely restricted at all.

06Heat

Heat is how much official attention you have attracted. It is the thread that ties the rest of the game together, and it makes three things worse at once: customs inspect you more often, pirates find you more often, and bribes become less likely to work.

What you doHeat
Buy a ton of contraband+2
Surrender cargo to customs+1
Pay a fine+4
Offer a bribe that is refused+8
Resist an inspection+15
Every jump−1

There is no way to pay heat off. The only cure is time: jump, trade clean, and let it fall. A run of quiet jumps is worth making before a big smuggling trip, not after.

07Customs

You are inspected when you arrive at a port, and sometimes stopped in transit by a patrol. The chance depends on the world's law level, plus a fifth of your heat, and never exceeds 70%. A patrol stopping you in transit is half as likely to inspect as a port is.

WhereBase chance
Frontier, law 0–210%
Middle band, law 3–525%
Core, law 6 and up45%

If the hold is clean you are waved through. If it is not, you choose:

KeyChoiceWhat it costs
CComply40 credits a ton, the cargo, and 4 heat.
BBribe70% less your heat, never below 20%. It works and nothing happens at all; it fails and you pay double the fine, lose the cargo anyway, and take 8 heat.
SSurrenderThe cargo and 1 heat. No fine.
RResistA fight with a patrol cutter, which is tougher than a pirate, and 15 heat whichever way it goes.

Surrendering is usually right when you are caught cold: you lose the goods either way, and the fine and the heat are what hurt. Bribing is a gamble that gets worse exactly when you most want it to work, because the odds fall as your heat rises.

08Pirates and patrols

Every jump rolls for an encounter against the law level of where you are going. Heat is added to both the pirate and the patrol chance.

DestinationPiratePatrolOffer
Frontier, law 0–225%2%15%
Middle, law 3–512%8%15%
Core, law 6+3%20%12%

A trade offer is simply money — a cargo contract worth 50 to 200 credits, paid on the spot.

Nobody kills you outright. A fight costs cargo and hull, and the run ends only if the hull reaches zero.

KeyChoiceWhat happens
EEvade50% plus 3 for every ton of fuel aboard. A full tank is a fast ship.
FFightYour hull against theirs, each plus a roll of six. Win and they break off; lose and you take one or two hull and lose cargo.
BBribe200 credits, 60% less your heat, never below 15%.
RRunThe same odds as evading.
Fuel is armourEvading improves by 3% a ton, so a ship that has just filled her tanks escapes about three times in four. One that is nearly dry escapes about half the time — and cannot jump away afterwards either.

A core world repairs a battered hull for half what a frontier yard charges. It is also where your contraband is most likely to be found, which is the whole difficulty of the game in one sentence.

10The mortgage

You start owing 20,000 credits. Every eight jumps, 800 falls due and is taken automatically if you have it. The port screen always shows the balance and how many jumps remain before the next payment.

Miss one and you get a warning. Miss two and the ship is repossessed.

Paying only the minimum takes two hundred jumps. Paying down the principal with M after a good run clears it in a handful. That is the shape of the game: the minimum keeps you flying, the profits buy you out.

Keep 800 in handA fine, a bribe or a boarding can empty your account at exactly the wrong moment. Going into the eighth jump broke is how most ships are lost — not to pirates, but to the bank.

11Winning and losing

Clear the debt and the ship is yours. That is the end of the game and the only way to win it.

Three ways to lose

  • Your hull reaches zero in a fight.
  • You miss two payments and the bank takes her.
  • You are stranded — no fuel, nothing in the hold to sell, and not enough credits to buy a single ton.

The third is the quiet one. Nothing warns you. Do not spend your last credits on cargo you have no fuel to move.

12Tips

  • Run the core first. Electronics and machinery out of Coriolis Gate, Verity and Tannhauser pay well and legally, and the core is almost free of pirates. Build a few thousand before you go looking for trouble.
  • Buy the weapons license before the weapons. Nine systems forbid them. One inspection at a core world costs more than the license did.
  • Watch the market, not the good. Prices reroll every docking, so a 15% swing can be worth more than choosing a different cargo. If a price is poor, sell part of the hold and come back.
  • Fill the tank before a frontier run. Fuel is cheapest in the core, it is your escape chance in a fight, and there is nowhere to buy it out there.
  • Let heat fall before the big trip. Heat is added to the pirate chance and the inspection chance and taken off your bribe odds. Four or five clean jumps before a smuggling run is time well spent.
  • Surrender rather than bribe when you are hot. Bribe odds are 70% less your heat. At 30 heat it is a 40% chance of nothing against a 60% chance of double the fine and 8 more heat.
  • Pay the principal the moment you can. A thousand off the debt is worth more than a thousand in the account, because the account is what fines and pirates take.

13About this edition

SpaceTrader was written in 2026 for the Timex Sinclair 2068, in Sinclair BASIC with Z80 machine code for the display work. It is not a port of anything; the setting takes its shape from the Traveller role-playing game, whose free traders are also mortgaged.

How it uses the machine

The whole game runs in the TS2068's Extended Color Mode, a feature almost nothing in 1983 used. It gives one attribute byte for every 8×1 strip of pixels instead of every 8×8 block — eight times the vertical color resolution of a standard Spectrum display, at the cost of a second 6K bitmap.

That is why the system art and the text can share a screen without the colour clash this machine is otherwise known for, and why a rule under the art can be a single pixel tall. The mode is entered once at the start and never left, because the ROM routine that is supposed to leave it does not work.

The twenty system illustrations are 2,048-byte band images occupying the top third of the screen — a third the size of a full screen, and a third the loading time. The font is drawn for this program and deliberately leaves the top pixel row of every character empty, so the rule has a clear line to sit on.
